## COMPETITOR GAP

**What comps get wrong for our audience:**
- Creator / "beauty CEO" voice and performative enthusiasm
- Hashtag lists as filler instead of caption quality
- Canva dependency — we sell **copy**, not design templates
- Generic lists (50–150 lines) with no situational organization
- Ad-headline packs that sound like promotions, not weekly presence
- Fake urgency ("book before it's too late") and chain-brand vocabulary ("crafted with care," "cozy vibes")

**What is genuinely missing:**
- **Salon-specific situational captions** organized by real week (open chair, client moment, quiet day) — not just service-type buckets (color, cuts, extensions)
- **Owner voice** that sounds like a stylist typed it, not a social media manager
- **Conversion mechanics** (hooks, questions, specific details, earned booking invites) **without** influencer tone
- **Copy-only PDF** — no Canva account required, no ChatGPT prompts required

---

## PATTERN SYNTHESIS

**Adopt (mechanics, not wording):**
- Strong **first-line hooks** — curiosity, specific client moment, quiet admission ("slow week"); first sentence must earn the rest (Instagram shows ~125 characters before "more")
- **Rhythm mix** — from **Tier A real posts**, not SEO blogs; vary post types per comp checklist
- **Hook → short body → optional CTA** where the moment warrants it (availability, transformation pride) — not hook → generic tagline ("you'll know if we're your kind of salon")
- **Specificity** over slogans — what they came in wanting, the result, the chair, the regular
- **Questions** as engagement CTAs when natural ("Have you been in lately?")
- **Category mix** aligned to salon rhythm: availability, transformation/client moment, behind-the-chair, community, wins
- Roughly **15–20%** of pack with clear energy/celebration (openings, proud results) — per schema hype guidance

**Avoid:**
- 5-hashtag blocks per caption as core value
- "We LOVE our clients!" performative warmth
- Beauty-influencer / lifestyle-brand adjectives
- Long caption formulas that read like training manuals
- Padding count with near-duplicate promo lines

**Differentiate:**
- Industry-specific salon vocabulary used correctly (chair, client, the result, the color)
- Situation-first categories (KIT 04 brief), not service-menu categories
- Short gate for pack copy — not Brand Alchemy marketing voice or full platform walkers
- Zero Canva — open PDF, copy, post

## Stage 4 failure — owner-outcome drift (June 2026)

**Symptom:** Templates pass voice walkers and rhythm checks but fail the **mirror test** — they report salon operations, process, or owner satisfaction instead of client feeling/result.

**Root cause:** Over-correction for brochure drift + staccato. Rules were read as "don't be performative / don't explain emotion" → safe owner-process copy. We skipped Check 3 as a real gate.

**Flagged examples → direction (not final copy):**

| # | Problem | Rewrite toward |
|---|---------|----------------|
| 13 | Process ("questions before result") not comfort + outcome | Nervous client → mirror → relief / "exactly what I wanted" |
| 16 | Closes on "the whole job" (owner) | Close on client words or visible result |
| 17 | "Didn't pitch us" = posting meta | Sister's nervous → same smile at mirror (referral through result) |
| 23 | Marketing salon vibe ("silent spa") | Show belonging/being known through client experience, not brand positioning |
| 28 | Ops win (on-time schedule) | Client-visible result worth showing — transformation, quote, parking-lot photo |

**Schema updates applied:** Template gate step 0 (mirror test), watchout #10, Outcome anchor (replaces Energy-only anchor), stricter Check 3.

**Next:** Rewrite Categories 3–6 (and audit 1–2) against mirror test before PDF v1 ships.
