## 1. Positioning principles (locked)

These decisions govern every section of the page.

### What we sell

**Brand strategy on paper + personalized messaging** — not a template pack, not auto-posting, not a logo mill.

The Identity Kit gives owner-operators:
- A clear brand foundation (voice, look, rollout plan)
- Paste-ready messaging built from their intake (bios, caption starters, CTAs)
- Documents they own and reuse for years

### Who we speak to (inclusive, no picker)

Primary audience from [TARGET_AUDIENCE.md](TARGET_AUDIENCE.md):
- Cafés and coffee shops
- Restaurants, bars, and bakeries
- Salons, spas, and beauty services
- Closely related: boutiques, gift shops, and maker goods businesses

**Do not** use an industry picker or tab UI on the sales page. Picking one vertical makes everyone else feel secondary.

**Do** name all buckets naturally in subcopy, show mixed proof in one gallery, and use universal owner-operator pain (voice drift, marketing as a second job).

### Pass test for every section

> Would a café owner, salon owner, restaurant owner, and boutique owner each see themselves here — without being asked to choose?

If only one vertical fits, broaden the example or add a second in the same section.

### What we do not benchmark against

| Comp type | Why not primary benchmark |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| Etsy / Canva template packs ($15–50) | Volume and price — we lose on quantity |
| Salon social kits ($147, 100 posts) | Post count — we are not selling 100 graphics |
| Feedo / AI posting tools ($9–49/mo) | Automation and visuals — different product category |
| Looka / logo generators | Visual-only — we lead with voice and strategy |

Acknowledge these in FAQ or one "more than templates" block. Do not structure the page to compete on their terms.

| Product | Price | What they sell | Gap vs Identity Kit |

| [Digital Launchpad](https://www.thewolfe.co/digital-launchpad) | Varies | Website + social templates in Canva | DIY templates; not intake-driven personalization |

### Cross-industry pattern (what wins in this market)

1. **Industry named in marketing** — but we achieve this via plural copy and mixed imagery, not a picker
2. **Operational outcomes** — "IG bio ready," "stop posting at midnight," not "PDF deliverable"
3. **Visual proof** — mockups, grids, before/after; our gap is we under-invest here today
4. **Price anchor** — vs agency, vs monthly tools, vs "one appointment" (salon comps use this well)
5. **Pain is visceral** — inconsistent voice, unrecognized brand, Canva at midnight

### Identity Kit differentiation (one sentence)

Template packs give you graphics that look like everyone else's. Posting tools polish photos but don't tell you who you are. **Identity Kit is a brand foundation** — who you serve, how you sound, how you look, and what to say — built from your answers.

## 4. Recommended page architecture

No industry picker. Single scroll story.

```
1. Hero
   - Headline: brand clarity (existing line works)
   - Sub: name all hospitality buckets once
   - Visual: mosaic or slow crossfade (PDF spread + mixed contexts)
   - CTA: Start my Identity Kit (price shown separately — one-time · $149)

2. Pain (universal owner-operator)
   - Voice drift, marketing as second job, work not showing online
   - No single-industry examples only

3. Before/after voice (differentiator)
   - Two rows: café caption + salon caption (or café + boutique product line)
   - Generic template vs kit output — same layout

4. What you'll use this week
   - Operational mapping (bio, new item post, review reply, visual direction, rollout)
   - Industry-agnostic tasks

5. Proof gallery
   - 4–6 tiles, subtle industry tags (Café · Salon · Restaurant · Boutique)
   - Real PDF spreads / paste-ready blocks from fulfillment samples

6. How it works
   - Keep 3 steps; add intake screenshot + delivery visual

7. What's in the kit
   - Foundation docs (4 PDFs) + personalized messaging
   - Secondary to outcomes; not the lead

8. More than templates (short)
   - Strategy positioning vs template packs and posting tools

9. For you / not for you
   - Reuse DigitalProductPage pattern

10. Offer block
    - $149, feature summary, single CTA
    - Soft anchor: strategy vs agency, or vs monthly posting tools

11. FAQ (5–7 questions)

12. Final CTA (one block — remove duplicate CTAs)
```

### Section priority for implementation

| Phase | Sections | Depends on |
|-------|----------|------------|
| A | Pain, before/after voice, offer + FAQ | Copy only; can ship with placeholder voice examples |
| B | Proof gallery, hero mosaic | PDF export assets from identity-kit repo |
| C | "What you'll use this week," for you/not for you | Content model in `content/identityKit.ts` |

## 5. Copy direction

### Hero

**Headline (keep or refine):**
> Stop guessing how your business should sound and look.

**Subline (inclusive):**
> For cafés, restaurants, salons, and boutiques — a personalized brand kit with your voice, visual direction, and messaging you can use the same week.

**Badge:** AI-enhanced (descriptor only — not "Pro")

### Pain block (draft)

Universal framing — no single vertical:

- You know your business. Your posts don't always show it.
- One week you sound professional; the next, generic. Customers can't tell it's you.
- You're great at the work. Marketing still feels like a second job.

### Before/after voice (draft examples)

Use real kit output when available; structure below is placeholder tone.

**Café — generic vs kit:**
- Generic: "✨ New menu item alert! Come try our amazing new latte! #coffee #cafelife"
- Kit: "Sitting with this one. New seasonal latte — cardamom and oat. On the board this week."

**Salon — generic vs kit:**
- Generic: "Book your appointment today! We can't wait to see you! 💇‍♀️✨"
- Kit: "If you're thinking about a change, we talk it through first. No surprises."

### "More than templates" block (draft)

> Template packs give you graphics that look like everyone else's. AI posting tools can polish a photo but won't tell you who you are. Identity Kit is different: a **brand foundation** — who you serve, how you sound, how you look, and what to say — built from your answers, not a generic grid.

### Price anchor options (pick one per context, do not stack)

- Less than a fraction of what a brand agency charges — personalized to your business
- Yours to keep; not another monthly posting subscription
- For salon owners: about the cost of one color appointment (use only in salon-adjacent ad creative, not necessarily on page)

### Headline variants (for later A/B)

1. Stop guessing how your business should sound and look. *(current)*
2. Your brand on paper — voice, look, and what to say.
3. Sound like your business, not a template.
4. The brand foundation for owners who run the place themselves.

## 7. FAQ (objections to address)

| Question | Answer direction |
|----------|------------------|
| Is this just Canva templates? | No — personalized documents from your intake; not a generic editable grid |
| How is this different from 100-post social kits? | Those sell volume; we sell your voice, look, and strategy on paper |
| I already have a logo — do I need this? | Yes — logo is one piece; kit covers voice, visual direction, and what to say |
| What types of businesses is this for? | Cafés, restaurants, salons, boutiques — owner-operated hospitality and related retail |
| What if I have a physical location? | Yes — voice and messaging for nearby discovery; local kits for Google/Yelp listing help |
| Is this a subscription? | No — one-time $149; documents are yours to keep |

## 8. For you / not for you

### For you

- You run a café, restaurant, salon, or boutique and post (or write) for the business yourself
- You want clarity on voice and look without hiring an agency
- You're tired of sounding different on every channel

### Not for you

- You only want a logo file
- You want someone to run your marketing every month
- You're an agency shopping for white-label assets
