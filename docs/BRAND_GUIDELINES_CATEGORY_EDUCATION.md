## What each reference does well

### Looka

- **“Rule book” framing** — Guidelines as shareable rules that make consistency easier (company of 1 or 100).
- **When do I use brand guidelines?** — Designing assets, working with a designer/printer, onboarding someone, growing the business.
- **Pared-down is fine** — Startups don’t need enterprise depth; add over time.
- **Consistency → trust → revenue** — Cites ~23% revenue lift from consistent presentation (same family as Lucidpress/Marq stats already in our branding article).

**Weaker for us:** Logo-first, designer-centric (clear space, CMYK, unacceptable uses). Big-brand examples (Slack, Uber) — wrong audience.

### Venngage

- **Three-way taxonomy** — **Brand guide** (rules) vs **brand kit** (asset files) vs **brand book** (comprehensive guide). Owners confuse these terms.
- **“Why it matters” + “Good example”** per section — Strong teaching pattern for articles or sales copy.
- **Decision matrix** — “If you need X, choose Y” (one-page vs simple vs small business, etc.).
- **Format comparison** — PDF vs slides vs editable online vs asset kit.
- **Voice as core section** — Not optional; equal to logo/colors/type in a serious guide.
- **Brand positioning before full guide** — Maps to our **Brand Brief**.
- **Hospitality examples** — Coffee shop, restaurant templates in the gallery (rare in this category).
- **Closing frame** — Goal is a document that gets **used**, not filed away.

**Weaker for us:** 70-template roundup (positions next to Canva/Venngage). Design-system depth (Pantone, accessibility contrast tables). Enterprise/tech example sections.

## What to borrow vs skip

**Borrow (patterns, not prose):**

- Rule book / master document language
- Guide ≠ kit ≠ book definitions
- “Why it matters / good example” per section
- One-page cheat sheet for Style Guide
- “Start simple, expand later”
- Restaurant/café/salon examples
- “Document gets used, not filed” outcome frame

**Skip:**

- Template gallery / “pick a template and customize” workflow
- Logo-first sales story (we lead with voice)
- Enterprise examples (Slack, Netflix, Mailchimp)
- Design-system depth (clear space math, Pantone, icon libraries) as lead content
- Positioning next to Looka/Venngage on their terms

## Copy snippets (draft seeds — Voice 1, not shipped)

**Guide vs kit (FAQ-style):**

> A logo file is an asset. A brand guide is the rule book for how you look and sound. Identity Kit gives you both the rules and the messaging to use them — built from your answers, not a generic template.

**When you’ll use this:**

> When someone new posts for you. When you rewrite your bio. When you hand something to a sign shop or printer. When you need three lines for a Thursday special without starting from scratch.

**One-page enough:**

> You don’t need Airbnb’s brand portal. You need a short cheat sheet plus voice rules you can hand to whoever posts on Tuesday.
